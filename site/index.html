Spotify on Windows, without the weight.

Openify is a fast, quiet desktop player for your Spotify Premium account. It is written in Rust and drawn natively: no web view, no Electron.

Measured on the author's machine with a small library. Your numbers will vary with library size and hardware. Pages you have opened before load from a local cache and appear at once while they refresh.

A calm room for your music.

The interface steps back. The cover that is playing tints the player and the full-screen view; everything else stays quiet.

Keyboard first

Ctrl K opens search from anywhere: a top result, songs, artists, albums and playlists from your library and Spotify, and a few actions when you type their name.

  • Space, Ctrl ← → and Ctrl ↑ ↓ control playback
  • ? shows every shortcut
  • Media keys, the tray icon, the Windows media overlay and an optional global search hotkey

Synced lyrics, with a translation

Lyrics follow the song in the side panel or full screen. They come from Spotify, with LRCLIB as a fallback, and an optional translation can sit under every line when one is available.

Equalizer and headphone profiles

A 10-band equalizer with presets and a preamp. Search your headphone model in the open OPRA database and get a ready-made correction curve fitted to the ten bands.

  • Gapless playback on albums
  • Optional crossfade from 2 to 12 seconds
  • Volume normalization, quality up to 320 kbps

Tools for a tidy library

Find duplicate songs and different versions of the same song (remaster, live, radio edit) in a playlist. Merge or split playlists, sort one permanently, back everything up to a JSON file and see your own listening statistics.

  • Undo library edits with Ctrl Z
  • Edits made offline are queued and sent when you are back online

Smarter shuffle and queue

Play one version of each song, skip explicit songs, never play a chosen artist, or start a radio from a song, artist, album or playlist.

Dark, Light or System

Keep the dark room, choose a warm off-white palette, or follow Windows app mode as it changes.

Spotify Connect

Openify shows up as a device in the Spotify app on your phone, so you can send music to it. You can rename it or turn it off.

English and Turkish

The whole interface is available in both languages.

Updates itself

Updates download in the background and apply the next time you open Openify. If a new version fails to start twice, it goes back to the previous one.

Private session

One switch in the profile menu stops Openify from recording recent plays and statistics for that session.

Install in a minute.

Openify installs for your user only. It does not need administrator rights.

  1. Download the installer

    Pick the one that matches your PC. Most PCs are x64; Surface Pro X and other Snapdragon laptops are ARM64.

    Installer, x64 Installer, ARM64

  2. Run it

    The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC".

    Click More info, then Run anyway. The source code and SHA-256 checksums of every file are on the release page.

  3. Sign in with Spotify

    Openify opens and a browser window asks you to sign in to your Spotify account. Your session is kept in the Windows Credential Manager.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, x64 or ARM64
  • A Spotify Premium account. Free accounts cannot stream through third-party clients.
  • A working audio output

Good to know

  • Openify is early (0.1). Friend activity and artist following are experimental.
  • Screen readers are not supported yet: the UI toolkit has no Windows accessibility support. Keyboard use is well covered.
  • Windows only. There are no macOS or Linux builds.

Your data stays on your PC.

There is no Openify server, no analytics and no telemetry.

Stored locally

The library cache, play history and statistics live in your user folder; the Spotify session lives in the Windows Credential Manager.

Plays are not reported to Spotify

Songs you play in Openify may not appear in Spotify's Recently played, your Wrapped or artists' play counts. Openify keeps its own history and statistics on your PC.

Only the services a feature needs

Besides Spotify: LRCLIB for missing lyrics, Deezer for similar artists, the OPRA database for headphone profiles and the update server.
